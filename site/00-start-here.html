It does not replace an accountant, a lawyer, or the rules in your own state. When the stakes are real (money, contracts, people's livelihoods), verify with someone licensed.

The one-paragraph version

You want to hire your first contractor (freelancer) or part-timer. You do not have an HR department — you are the HR department. This guide walks you through the whole arc in a humane order:

  1. Decide what kind of help you actually need (Ch. 1)
  2. Write a posting that doesn't mislead (Ch. 2)
  3. Pick someone using a paid trial project, not vibes alone (Ch. 3)
  4. Pay them plainly and on time (Ch. 4)
  5. Keep just enough records (Ch. 5)
  6. Onboard them without drowning them in info (Ch. 6)
  7. End the work kindly and clean up access (Ch. 7)
  8. Keep yourself from drowning in guidance (Ch. 8)

Everything is designed to be done by one person, on evenings and weekends, without special software.

A note before you start: you're not behind

Most hiring advice is written for companies hiring employee #50. The truth for your first hire is simpler:

  • You can be small and still be fair and professional. Kind + clear beats polished + jargon-y, every time.
  • The paperwork side is learnable, not scary. Chapter 4 and 5 are the only "paperwork" chapters, and they're deliberately short.
  • You're allowed to start with something tiny. A 6-week trial project is a fine first hire. It counts. You'll learn more from it than from 20 articles.

How to actually use this guide

  • Read Chapter 1 first. It's the fork in the road: everything else depends on what kind of help you decided you need.
  • The extras at the end (interview-scorecard.md, first-week-onboarding-sheet.md, glossary.md) are meant to be used, not read cover-to-cover. Print them, fill them in, mark them up.
  • Worked examples use a tiny fictional studio ("a solo author who hires a part-time helper to sort podcast transcripts") because concrete beats abstract.

The "I'm overwhelmed" escape hatch

If you retain nothing else: do the boring next thing. Write the posting. Reply to the one promising applicant. Pay the invoice that's due. The whole guide compresses down to: be clear, pay on time, write things down, say thank you. Everything else is recovery from skipping one of those four.
