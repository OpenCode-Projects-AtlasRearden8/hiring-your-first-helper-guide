# Chapter 2: Writing the Posting

*Education only, never legal or tax advice.*

## The core rule: no bait

The only unforgivable posting sin is a mismatch. If the posting says one thing
and day one reality is another, you've broken trust on both sides and wasted
everyone's month. Every rule below serves that one rule.

## The anatomy of an honest posting

A good first-hire posting fits on one screen. Use these five blocks:

### 1. The plain-language title

Bad: "Growth Ninja / Rockstar VA / Opportunity Seeker."
Good: "Part-Time Podcast Transcripts Sorter — 4–6 hrs/week."

The title describes the actual work, the commitment, and nothing added.

### 2. What you actually need (the work)

Three to five bullets, present tense, written like Instructions For Use:

```
* Listen to my podcast episodes and mark guest Briggs moments with timestamps
* Clean up auto-generated transcripts and save them with the naming pattern I provide
* Post a short weekly recap into my shared folder  (\~1 hour total)
```

If you can't write this yet, do **the work yourself a few times first** and
write down what you're doing. That list becomes the posting — and later, the
training material (Chapter 6).

### 3. Who it's for, stated as a human being

"Someone patient with repetitive-but-calm work, comfortable with [tool], takes
pride in tidy files." Skip the personality-label bingo ("detail-oriented
self-starter"). Describe the *working conditions* and let that speak.

### 4. The money, out loud

**Always put the pay in the posting.** People without HR experience assume a
missing number is a red flag, and honestly it usually is. State:

- whether it's hourly or flat fee,
- the rate (exact numbers, not "depends on experience"),
- how often you'll pay, and by what day the invoice should land for it.

If you'd like *feedback on your number*, you can phrase it ("$18–22/hr depending
on experience") but never make the pay itself a mystery.

### 5. Logistics, logistics, logistics

- Remote or local? If local: what city, and is there any reason to meet in person?
- Weekly hours (a rough range is fine).
- Duration — trial-project first? (Chapter 3 says: yes.)
- How to apply, stated simply: "Email dylan@example.com with two sentences
  about why this looks like your kind of work, plus a sample of something
  similar you've done."
- And the truest sentence of all: "**Independent contractor** arrangement; I
  won't withhold employment taxes." (That's honesty in a posting, not a legal
  determination — Chapter 1's caveat still applies.)

## Finding where to post

Match the pond to the fish:

- **Task-y one-offs** → gig boards/local community boards.
- **Skilled recurring** → communities where that skill already hangs out (an
  editing-for-authors Facebook group; a local college's career services).
- **People who'd love a quiet second gig** → nurses, teachers, librarians,
  admins' existing networks often already overlap with what you need.
- **Your own circle** — genuinely underrated. Past students, past clients, a
  book club friend who has these exact skills. (Hire warmly but *definitely*
  still do the paid trial.)

## Red flags on the application side

You'll also be *receiving* applications; here's what applicants hate seeing:

- "Send your full work history and a 500-word essay" for 4 hrs/week.
- "Will pay in exposure" or vaguely "equity."
- "Must be available 24/7."
- No name on the business, no explanation who's hiring.

The golden move: your own posting proves what dealing with you will be like.
**Make it generous, clear, and short.** It filters whom.

## One worked posting (from the fictional example)

```
**Seeking: Part-Time Podcast Support (4–6 hrs/week, remote, paid trial)**

Hi — I'm Dylan; I write plain-English books and guides about AI. My workflow
creates weekly podcast episodes and I need help with the *aftermath*.

Work involved, roughly 4–6 flexible hours/week:
* Clean up auto-generated episode transcripts
* Timestamp guest highlights and export a recap
* File everything in a shared folder with a simple naming system
* Very occasional email drafting (I give you a template)

Pay: $XX/hr, paid weekly by invoice — invoices processed by Friday. Flat trial
project will be paid, too. Independent contractor role.

Me: I'm patient, async, reply within one business day, and I will supply a
concrete training doc in week one because I dislike guessing games as much
as you do.

Apply: email me 2 sentences about why this fits you + a sample of a
transcript file you've cleaned up (any format).
```

That whole ad did this manual's entire job: it made expectations explicit, so
surprises on both sides are structural rather than constant.
