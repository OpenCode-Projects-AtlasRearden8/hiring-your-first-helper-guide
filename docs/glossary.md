# Glossary (Plain-English Edition)

Definitions are *plain-English explanations for orientation*, not legal
definitions. When money or the law is actually at stake, this word helps you
ask professionals better questions — it doesn't replace them.

## The people and the arrangement

**Contractor (independent contractor, freelancer)** — someone who works *for
you but not under you*: they choose how and often when they work, use their
own tools, serve multiple clients, and bill you with invoices. The legal test
is about the working relationship, not the paperwork label.

**Employee** — someone who works *within your business*: your schedule, your
direction, your equipment, and substantially heavier legal obligations on your
side (payroll withholding, unemployment insurance, labor rules). Don't drift
into this shape while calling it "contractor."

**Client** — as used in the companion guide *freelance-first-client-guide*,
the person/entity doing the paying. You are the client in this guide; your
contractor is the freelancer in that one.

**Part-timer** — colloquially, someone working a small number of hours per
week. The legal name of the arrangement (contractor vs. employee) matters more
than the hour count.

## Money words

**Rate** — the per-hour (or per-unit) price agreed before work begins.
Written down. No mystery.

**Flat fee** — one price for a defined piece of work, regardless of hours.
Great when scope is crystal clear.

**Retainer** — a fixed recurring payment for a standing amount of work per
month (e.g., "$400/month covers up to 8 hours"). Requires an up-front
conversation about what "inside" the retainer is.

**Invoice** — the bill *they* send *you*: who, what work, what amount, how to
pay. Your payment log and invoice PDFs are your proof you paid.

**W-9** — a one-page US form the contractor fills out *for you* before you pay
them: their legal name, address, and taxpayer ID. You keep it on file. That's
its whole job at your scale.

**1099-NEC** — the year-end US form on which you generally report contractor
payments of $600 or more in a year. Your job: collect W-9s and track totals;
your accountant's job: confirm and file.

**Withholding** — money an *employer* sets aside from an employee's pay for
taxes. Not something you do for genuine contractors (they handle their own
taxes from what you pay them).

**Net-15 / net-30** — when an invoice is due: 15 or 30 days from the invoice
date. Inside this guide you'll usually promise something faster and kinder,
like "paid by the following Tuesday."

## Work-shaping words

**Scope** — what the work *is* and, just as importantly, what it *isn't*.
Scope creep is when "can you also…" grows silently until the pay no longer
matches the work.

**Trial project** — a small, *paid* slice of real work used to test fit on
both sides. Never unpaid; see Ch. 3.

**Deliverable** — the concrete thing the work produces (a cleaned
transcript, a working page, a file renamed to the system). Helps "done" mean
something checkable.

**Milestone** — a checkpoint splitting larger work into payable,
reviewable chunks.

**Working agreement** — this guide's plain-language, in-writing statement of
rate, schedule, independence, ending terms, and ownership. Not legal advice;
just shared clarity.

**Conflict of interest** — when the person's work for you competes with other
work they do (e.g., also editing your competitor's podcast). Usually fine;
always fine when said out loud.

## Process words

**Onboarding** — everything that happens in the first days so a person can do
real work without feeling lost. In this guide: staged, minimal, no flooding.

**Offboarding** — ending the work: the human goodbye *and* the access cleanup
(folders, logins, passwords). Two events; keep them separate but same-day.

**Check-in** — a small, regular contact point (e.g., Monday "what I did /
what's next" note). Prevention for both silence and surprise.

**Confidentiality (at your scale)** — a promise not to share each other's
private business info publicly. Not a corporate NDA apparatus.

## One-line version of this whole glossary

> Contractor, not employee; written rate, real invoice, W-9 on file, pay on
> time; paid trial before commitment; small scope, plain agreement; say the
> ending out loud; revoke access the same day.
