# Interview Scorecard

Print one copy per candidate. Fill it *during or immediately after* the
conversation — fresh impressions beat remembered ones. The score buys you
consistency across candidates, not a robot decision; a strong gut signal
up or down is worth noting even if the arithmetic disagrees.

## Three gut-check questions (answer in one sentence each)

1. Would I be comfortable sending this person a task and not thinking about it
   for two days? (Yes / Probably / No)
2. Did anything they said or did actively *warm* me to them specifically?
   (Write it down — a "nothing" is informative.)
3. Did anything raise a genuine flag? (e.g., the way they talked about a
   former client, vagueness about availability, a rate conversation that felt
   off.)

## Decision rule of thumb

- **28+ and no gut flags** → proceed to the paid trial project (Ch. 3), not to a
  hiring decision. The trial is the real interview.
- **20–27** → maybe, if one specific weak trait is either trainable or
  irrelevant to your actual work. Be honest about which.
- **<20** → pass, kindly (Ch. 3 has the templates).

## Copy-paste interview questions (the whole list)

- "Walk me through one piece of work you're actually proud of — and why."
- "What would you do if you got stuck and I wasn't responding for a day?"
- "How do you like to receive feedback: written, live, both?"
- "What hours do you actually want to work? (The true answer, not the
  polite one.)"
- "What questions do *you* have for me about the work or about me?"
  (A blank here is itself informative.)
